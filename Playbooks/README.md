# Playbooks

Playbooks are on-demand guidance for recurring kinds of work.

A playbook should be read only when its task is relevant. It should explain the
purpose, inputs, desired output, useful construction guidance, and important
failure modes for that kind of work.

Playbooks do not need to prescribe an agent's internal reasoning. They should
state the outcome and the evidence or properties that matter, then trust the
agent to choose an effective path.

| Playbook | Read it when |
|---|---|
| [Grounding the Seed](grounding-the-seed.md) | Turning the seed idea into a bounded, source-grounded investigation |
| [Writing the Research Contract](writing-the-research-contract.md) | Building or sharpening `Research/Research Contract.md` |
| [Running the Investigation](running-the-investigation.md) | Building, measuring, analyzing, and interpreting under an active contract |
| [Reporting the Result](reporting-the-result.md) | Producing the outputs the contract promised |
| [Asking for a Focused Improvement](asking-for-a-focused-improvement.md) | Another participant's perspective would materially improve important work |

Project-wide research standards, stages, change rules, and completion
conditions live in `Project Details/Project Details.md`. Playbooks add craft
guidance for a specific task; they do not restate those standards.

Add a playbook when a kind of work recurs and its guidance is worth more than
the cost of reading it. Otherwise, trust the team.
