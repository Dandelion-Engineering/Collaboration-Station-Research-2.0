# Asking for a Focused Improvement

Read this when another participant's perspective would materially improve
important work.

A focused improvement is one contribution, in one direction. The responsible
agent asks, the contributor answers once, the responsible agent decides what to
use and continues. There is no return leg.

## When it is worth asking

- an artifact whose defects would be expensive to discover later;
- a method or interpretation where a different reading of the field matters;
- a result you cannot find a flaw in but do not yet trust;
- a difficult problem where a second approach may simply be better;
- an independent implementation, counterexample, or missing baseline.

It is not worth asking on routine work, and it is not something every artifact
needs.

## Ask against a stable object

The artifact or decision must be settled enough that a contribution can be
complete. Asking for improvements on something still being designed produces a
conversation that cannot end, because each answer changes what was being
improved.

If the work is still moving, ask for design help in a chat instead. That is a
useful and different thing.

## Ask for everything at once

Say what the artifact is for, who reads it, and what constraints it must
satisfy — then ask for everything the contributor finds, including minor points.

A request to be conservative or to report only serious problems will be followed
literally, and you will get less than the contributor saw. Ask broadly and
filter yourself afterward. One thorough pass is the whole mechanism; a sequence
of narrow ones is how a single contribution becomes nine.

## Contributing to someone else's work

Read the artifact as a whole against a written statement of what it is for.
Write that purpose down before you start, and judge whether the artifact
accomplishes it — not whether it changed in the ways someone previously
suggested, and not against a previous draft.

Give your best assessment plainly, including uncertainty. You are not
certifying the work, and nothing waits on you.

## What happens next

The responsible agent decides what improves the work, applies it, records
material uncertainty where it affects the result, and moves forward.

A contribution creates no obligation to defend the artifact, implement every
suggestion, return a revised version, report back on what was adopted, or seek
agreement. There is no second pass on the same artifact — if later work reveals
a problem, that is ordinary forward correction, handled where it is found.

## When you disagree

Participants do not need identical beliefs to proceed. When evidence settles the
question, follow it. When it does not, choose the most honest and most
reversible path, record the uncertainty where it affects the result or a future
decision, and continue.

The researcher does not need to arbitrate technical disagreement between
agents. When a research preference would materially improve fit, ask an
advisory question with a live default. When action needs human-only authority,
ask an authority question whose default is not to act. Record either kind in
`Research/Questions.md` and continue on the available path.
